Payroll Tax Disputes in California

A payroll tax problem can involve more than an employer’s unpaid balance. The agency may question deposits, return information or who was responsible for withheld funds. Business owners, officers and employees need to understand whether the inquiry concerns the company, an individual or both.

RV Litigation Group PC represents businesses and individuals in payroll tax controversies. This is tax-dispute representation concerning withholding, assessments and agency review. It is separate from employee claims against an employer. Our employment-litigation practice represents businesses and employers.

Recognize Potential Criminal Overlap

If the inquiry includes allegations of intentional tax misconduct, the matter may also require Criminal Tax Defense. Statements and records should be handled with that overlap in mind. An unpaid payroll balance or routine adjustment does not by itself establish a criminal offense.

Responsibility and the Willfulness Standard Under Section 6672

The Trust Fund Recovery Penalty generally concerns the unpaid trust-fund portion, including specified taxes withheld from employees; it is not personal liability for every component of the company’s tax bill. Responsibility and willfulness are distinct requirements. More than one person may have responsibility, and exclusive or ultimate control is not required.

Review authority to direct payments, access accounts, sign checks, supervise payroll and choose creditors. A title or ownership percentage alone is not decisive. Delegating payroll duties may not relieve a person who retained sufficient authority. The timeline matters: someone genuinely new to management presents a different inquiry from a person who always retained control but later resumed daily oversight.

Willfulness for this noncriminal penalty can involve knowing preference of other creditors over unpaid trust-fund taxes or reckless disregard of whether the taxes were paid. It does not require an evil motive, and it should not be equated with the separate elements and burden in a criminal prosecution. Counsel examines when the individual learned of the problem, what power existed and what payments followed. See Purcell v. United States, 1 F.3d 932 (9th Cir. 1993).

Personal assessments also require attention to representation and conflicts. The company and different decision-makers may have different interests. We clarify the client and review interview requests, proposed assessments and appeal instructions before responding on anyone’s behalf.

Separate the IRS and EDD Procedural Tracks

The IRS offers a pre-assessment opportunity to appeal a proposed trust-fund penalty through its notice process. Preserve that opportunity by following the particular letter’s instructions and applicable deadline; an IRS payroll examination, individual penalty and EDD assessment do not share one universal procedure.

For an IRS proposed personal assessment, preserve the proposed-assessment letter, supporting interview record and administrative protest information. After assessment, a TFRP refund suit may involve the divisible-tax exception to the usual full-payment rule: the required portion and administrative claim must be identified for each period. It is not a general permission to pay an arbitrary small amount and sue.

An EDD assessment may be challenged by a petition to CUIAB under the applicable California Unemployment Insurance Code procedure. Ordinary and jeopardy assessments have different filing rules; service and available extensions require review. Filing a petition does not itself stop interest. A federal resolution does not settle an EDD assessment.

EDD Worker Classification: Identify the Test First

An EDD assessment can involve unemployment contributions, employment training tax, disability withholding, income-tax withholding, interest or penalties. The work, period and any statutory exception must be identified before selecting the classification test. A Form 1099 or independent-contractor label does not settle the question.

When the ABC test governs, the business must establish all three conditions: freedom from its control both under the contract and in practice; work outside its usual course of business; and an independently established trade, occupation or business of the same nature. These are separate requirements. Evidence of other clients does not cure a failure to establish that the work is outside the company’s usual business.

A statutory exception may require the multifactor Borello test or another rule; it does not automatically make the worker a contractor. Under Borello, the right to control and relevant secondary factors matter together. Examine retained contractual rights as well as actual supervision, payment terms, tools, hiring authority, business organization and opportunity for profit or loss. Read California’s explanation of the tests and exceptions.

An ordinary assessment under Unemployment Insurance Code section 1222 generally has a 30-day petition period after service. Any good-cause extension requires the applicable determination; it is not automatic extra time. Preserve the notice and service facts and distinguish jeopardy procedures. The CUIAB route is separate from an IRS dispute and does not itself stop interest.

Missing Deposits, Wrong Quarters and Payroll Providers

Build a quarter-by-quarter reconciliation of the return liability, payroll register, bank debit, deposit confirmation and IRS account posting. Confirm the employer identification number, form, tax period, amount and effective payment date. A transfer to a payroll provider does not by itself prove the IRS received the deposit. Equally, an agency balance may require correction if an actual payment was posted to the wrong period.

Separate correction of a payment record from relief from a valid late-deposit penalty. Preserve the provider agreement, reports, instructions and communications, and respond to the notice while records are traced. Ordinary outsourcing does not eliminate employer obligations. A qualifying certified professional employer organization arrangement can change the federal treatment under section 3511, so identify the provider’s actual status, contract and covered workers rather than treating every payroll service alike.

Maintain current reporting and deposits during the dispute. See the IRS comparison of third-party payroll arrangements and section 3511.

Records That Help Us Evaluate the Matter

  • Forms 941 or other relevant federal returns, California payroll reports, wage details and deposit confirmations.
  • Agency transcripts or account histories, proposed and final assessments, audit schedules and prior responses.
  • Bank permissions, payroll-provider agreements, ownership or management changes, approval policies and creditor-payment records.
  • Communications showing when each person learned of a shortfall and what authority or corrective action was available.

Start an inquiry with the notice or allegation, your role and any stated deadline. Preserve complete originals; arrange an appropriate transfer method before sending sensitive financial records.

Fictional California Examples

These fictional examples illustrate issues counsel may evaluate. They are not firm cases or results. Locations identify the setting; they do not establish venue, a local legal rule or a firm office.

Fictional example: Chula Vista, San Diego County — earlier quarters and a new finance officer

A finance officer disputes a proposed TFRP covering periods before and after joining the company. Counsel compares appointment dates, retained and actual payment authority, bank access, knowledge of arrears and funds available at each stage. Neither the new title nor the assertion that the debt is old resolves all of those questions.

Fictional example: San Jose, Santa Clara County — implementation specialists

A software company treats implementation specialists as contractors, but their work may form part of its ordinary services. Counsel first checks the governing test and any exception. Under ABC, outside customers and project invoices cannot by themselves overcome a failure of the usual-course requirement.

Fictional example: Salinas, Monterey County — a provider payment does not match the IRS account

A company used a payroll provider and receives a balance-due notice. Bank withdrawals and provider reports show funds moved, but the IRS transcript does not credit the same quarter. Counsel traces the payment destination and identifiers, evaluates a correction or penalty issue, and reviews the provider arrangement without assuming it transfers every obligation.
