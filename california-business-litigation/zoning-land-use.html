Understanding Zoning & Land Use Law in California

A permit condition, use restriction or code-enforcement notice can affect whether a property serves its intended purpose. Land use review starts with the actual parcel, decision, governing plans and procedural history, not a county-wide assumption about what is permitted.

RV Litigation Group PC advises California property owners and businesses on zoning disputes, permits, variances, land use restrictions and related administrative or litigation issues. We evaluate the legal route alongside the client’s project and any appropriate planning or technical advisers.

The Legal Framework

Local rules operate within state law

General plans, zoning ordinances, specific plans, existing approvals and state legislation can each affect a use. A variance and a conditional use permit serve different purposes and have different findings. A neighboring property’s approval does not automatically establish a right to the same project or exemption.

Environmental review has its own framework

CEQA may apply to discretionary agency approvals, subject to statutory and categorical exemptions and other requirements. An exemption, negative declaration, mitigated negative declaration and environmental impact report are distinct routes. Counsel must review the actual project and current law; neither the size of a project nor a preferred outcome determines the appropriate process.

Preserve the administrative route and deadlines

Comments, objections, administrative appeals and record preparation can affect later judicial review. Deadlines differ by decision and notice. A Notice of Determination and a Notice of Exemption have different consequences; the filing office does not by itself turn one deadline into the other. Some challenges require both timely filing and service. Do not rely on a generic 90-day period or assume negotiations extend review deadlines.

From the Permit Record to Judicial Review

An incomplete application and a permit denial differ

The immediate task is to identify the agency action: an application-completeness determination, a proposed condition, a hearing decision or a final denial. Local ordinances and the applicable state statute determine the available response. Government Code section 65943 includes a written appeal process for specified incompleteness determinations, with conditions that should not be treated as identical for every initial staff response.

Preserve issues through the administrative process

Available administrative review generally must be completed before court review. Objections should be presented at the required stages while the agency can address them, supported by the relevant documents and technical evidence. Staff reports, plans, notices, hearing materials, written findings and the final decision form the record. Exceptions to exhaustion are fact-specific; ongoing discussions do not justify assuming that an appeal can wait.

Choose the correct court proceeding

Administrative mandamus under Cal. Code Civ. Proc. section 1094.5 applies to qualifying decisions made after a proceeding in which a hearing and evidence are legally required and the agency exercises factual discretion. Other decisions may call for traditional mandate or a different statutory procedure. The review can concern fair procedure, findings and evidentiary support; the standard of review depends on the decision and applicable law. Court review is not simply a new permit application or a promise of project approval.

Facts & Records to Prepare

  • The property address and parcel information, zoning designation and relevant plans or ordinances.
  • The application, staff reports, conditions, hearing notices, final decision and proof of its issuance.
  • Environmental documents and filed notices, including dates and copies of comments or objections.
  • Prior permits, enforcement notices, administrative appeals and technical reports supporting the proposed use.

How We Approach the Matter

Identify the decision and next forum

Counsel can determine whether the immediate step is an application response, hearing, local appeal or court proceeding. The type of decision and available administrative remedies guide the process.

Build a usable record

Legal arguments should be supported by documents and appropriate technical evidence submitted through the required process. Relevant findings, inconsistencies and objections should be identified while the agency can address them. A later lawsuit may be limited by the record and issues properly raised.

Evaluate a workable project response

Revised plans, conditions, compliance measures or a negotiated administrative resolution may be considered alongside a challenge. Court review is not a guarantee of project approval.
