WELFARE & INSTITUTIONS CODE 15600 ET SEQ.

Elder Financial Abuse Allegations

Representation in Claims Under California's Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act

When a caregiver, family member, fiduciary, or acquaintance financially exploits an elderly or dependent adult, California law provides significantly enhanced civil remedies — including attorney fees and, in appropriate cases, punitive damages — that go beyond what is available in an ordinary civil claim.

What Is Elder Financial Abuse Under California Law?

Elder financial abuse occurs when a person or entity takes, secretes, appropriates, or retains real or personal property of an elder (age 65 or older) or dependent adult for a wrongful use or with intent to defraud, or through undue influence. It can involve a caregiver, family member, fiduciary, or a stranger, and often includes improper changes to estate planning documents, unauthorized withdrawals, or coerced transfers of property.

What the Law Says

Welfare & Institutions Code 15610.30 — Financial Abuse Defined

"'Financial abuse' of an elder or dependent adult occurs when a person or entity... takes, secretes, appropriates, obtains, or retains real or personal property of an elder or dependent adult for a wrongful use or with intent to defraud, or both..." — California Welfare & Institutions Code Section 15610.30(a)(1)

This statute also expressly covers taking property through undue influence, as defined under Welfare & Institutions Code 15610.70 — a legal standard that examines the vulnerability of the elder, the influencer's authority, tactics used, and the equity of the resulting outcome, even without traditional fraud.

Enhanced Remedies

"" —

Unlike most civil claims, a successful elder financial abuse claim under Welfare & Institutions Code 15657.5 can result in an award of attorney fees and costs to the prevailing plaintiff, and — where the defendant's conduct is shown to be reckless, oppressive, fraudulent, or malicious — an award of punitive damages, even after the elder's death.

Real-World Examples

Example 1 — Caregiver Exploitation in San Jose

A hired in-home caregiver gradually gains access to an elderly client's finances and makes a series of unauthorized transfers to her own accounts over several months. The family retains counsel to pursue a financial abuse claim and recover the misappropriated funds, plus attorney fees.

Example 2 — Undue Influence in Estate Planning in Palo Alto

A family member isolates an elderly parent from other relatives and arranges for a new will and power of attorney that dramatically favors the isolating family member shortly before the parent's decline. Counsel evaluates the undue influence factors — including the parent's vulnerability and the suddenness of the change — to challenge the transactions.

Example 3 — Contractor Fraud in Oakland

A contractor convinces an elderly homeowner to pay for home repairs that are never performed, or performed at a fraction of the agreed cost. Counsel pursues a financial abuse claim seeking full restitution plus the enhanced remedies available under the statute.
